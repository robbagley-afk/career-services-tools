PeopleGrove CES Hub Consolidation

Project managed a year-long migration consolidating four separate PeopleGrove hubs — BYU, BYU-Idaho, BYU-Hawaii, and Ensign College — into a single shared Church Educational System hub. Coordinated weekly vendor calls, a managed bug and feature-request backlog, administrative role design, and quarterly coordination council meetings from planning through launch.

Expanded the alumni network available to students from 7,000 to 75,000 contacts

VMock Resume Platform Implementation

Selected, implemented, and administered VMock, then authored the accompanying resume self-study modules. Automated hundreds of hours per year previously spent on manual resume formatting review.

Shifted resume review from master's-level staff to trained student employees and missionary volunteers without reducing service quality. Appointments per student rose 43 percent over the same period, so students received more contact, not less

Internship Approval Workflow Migration

Migrated internship approval off Smartsheet onto Power Automate and Power Apps, both already licensed to the college and unused, closing a compliance gap that put OPT eligibility at risk for international students.

Raised employer agreement compliance from 30% to 100%; retired a $1,500 annual license cost

Service Scale Outcomes, 2018–2025

Scaled department service delivery through process redesign, platform implementation, and training. Full-time staff was cut 25 percent, from four to three, and delivery was rebuilt around 14 half-time student employees who came to carry 70 percent of service hours. Cost per appointment fell by roughly half.

Annual one-on-one appointments grew more than tenfold, from 338 to 3,692, and unique students served rose from 13.7% to 42% of enrollment, with employment within one year of graduation held at 90%

AI Agents & Applications

Ten custom agents built for career services, tracked by actual adoption rather than launch count: three in regular use by career mentors, two embedded in The Job Search Workshop and CAR 201 and therefore reaching the student body, three built for narrower needs that see occasional use, and one, the Internship Expert, since upgraded from an internal agent to a standalone application now in internal prototype testing, demoable below under Live Applications. Several were built on a Microsoft Copilot Studio license the college already held and was not using. Live demos link to running applications; the remaining production Ensign agents are internal and described here rather than linked.

Local Inference Infrastructure

Proof-of-concept environment — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B served through LM Studio, LiteLLM as an OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible translation layer, and Tailscale for secure access — evaluating whether sensitive AI workloads can run on institutionally controlled infrastructure at no incremental licensing cost.

Every live demo above except the Academic Advisor runs on this infrastructure. The Internship Expert, Resume Coach, Resume Improver, and Interview Practice all perform inference locally on hardware the department already owns, with no per-seat licensing, no student data leaving controlled infrastructure, and no third-party model provider in the path. The Academic Advisor is cloud-primary with automatic local fallback, included as the deliberate contrast case.
